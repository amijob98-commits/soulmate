# Knowledge Libraries and Source Policy

## Parallel library structure
Maintain distinct but linkable libraries:
1. Psychology, psychiatry, trauma, and human development.
2. Jewish wisdom, Kabbalah, and Hasidut.
3. Palmistry (historical/interpretive; not validated personality diagnosis).
4. Face reading (historical/interpretive; not validated personality diagnosis).

6. Tongue reading / Traditional Chinese Medicine tongue observation (traditional interpretive method, not a medical diagnosis).

Do not collapse these into one undifferentiated evidence category. Cross-link ideas while preserving each source's tradition and epistemic status.

## Expanded professional and scientific human-knowledge library

SOULMATE's knowledge base must include professional and scientific literature about the human body, mind, brain, and behavior alongside Jewish wisdom, Kabbalah, Hasidut, and the project's interpretive traditions. This is a core library, not an optional supplement.

### Required research domains
- Clinical psychology: emotions, behavior, anxiety, recurring patterns, relationships, assessment, and change.
- Psychiatry: mental disorders, diagnostic frameworks, treatment approaches, and their limitations.
- Neuroscience: memory, emotion, decision-making, learning, stress systems, and habits.
- Trauma and attachment: effects of life experiences, relationships, development, and adversity on mind and body.
- Developmental psychology: human development from childhood through older age.
- Psychotherapy: therapeutic modalities, mechanisms of change, indications, limitations, and evidence quality.
- Body and mind: relationships among physical health, nervous-system processes, and emotional experience.
- Behavioral science: habits, motivation, reinforcement, addiction, decision-making, and behavior change.

### Four distinct knowledge layers
1. **Spiritual and textual sources** — what a specific religious or spiritual tradition and its primary texts teach about the person. Preserve source, author, tradition, and interpretive lineage.
2. **Professional and scientific sources** — what empirical research and professional literature report, including uncertainty, methodological limitations, and what remains unknown.
3. **Therapeutic approaches** — how practitioners work with difficulties and recurring patterns; distinguish established clinical practice from emerging, traditional, or unvalidated approaches.
4. **Synthesis** — compare and connect ideas across layers. Explicitly identify convergence, differences, tensions, and contradictions. Never present spiritual teachings as scientific findings, and never erase scientific evidence or uncertainty to force agreement.

### Evidence and provenance rules
For each claim or source, retain a source type and evidence status. Prefer primary research, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, clinical guidelines, and authoritative professional references for clinical/scientific claims. Record publication date and edition. Separate an author's interpretation from the underlying study results. Note disagreements and uncertainty rather than flattening them into a single answer.

## Rights and ingestion policy
- Ingest full text only when public domain or clearly licensed for the intended use.
- For copyrighted works without permission: catalog bibliographic metadata, link to lawful access, record rights status, and create independent summaries/notes only where permitted.
- Store provenance, author, exact title/edition, source URL, rights status, ingestion status, and page/section locators.
- Do not infer that a work has been ingested merely because it was discussed or uploaded in chat.

## Distinct Hasidic and contemporary methods: mirrors, Ein HaBedolach, and Nekudat Emet

These are related but distinct source traditions and must never be merged into one method or attributed to the same author.

### Baal Shem Tov: the teaching of mirrors (תורת המראות)
- Treat this as a Hasidic teaching whose primary sources, textual witnesses, context, and interpretive history must be researched directly.
- Study the original Hasidic sources and reliable editions/commentaries before summarizing the teaching.
- Do not reduce it to the slogan that everything a person dislikes in another person must necessarily exist within themselves. Preserve nuance, context, and alternative interpretations; do not turn the teaching into blame or invalidate a person's legitimate perception of another's harmful behavior.
- Record exact source passages and editions wherever possible.

### עין הבדולח — Rabbi Yuval HaCohen Asherov
- Catalog as a distinct contemporary method developed by Rabbi Asherov, drawing on the teaching of mirrors and applying it to emotional residues, patterns of response, personal responsibility, and inner work.
- Do not attribute the method itself directly to the Baal Shem Tov. Distinguish the historical Hasidic source from the contemporary systematization and its practical claims.
- Initial discovery links supplied by the user:
  - Background / method: https://www.besodh.com/אודות/
  - Course collection: https://www.besodh.com/course-category/עין-הבדולח/
  - Lesson on responsibility: https://www.hidabroot.org/video/210594
  - Additional discovery lead: https://www.ayalima.co.il/ (verify the exact relevant page before citing it).
- Research the method's own terminology, steps, assumptions, intended use, limitations, and examples. Distinguish its spiritual/therapeutic framework from clinically validated interventions.

### נקודת אמת — Rabbi Asher Eliyahu Garlitz
- Maintain as an independent method / knowledge section, not as a branch of עין הבדולח and not as a synonym for the Baal Shem Tov's teaching.
- Initial subject map:
  - identifying the inner statement or belief that activates a person;
  - identifying negative self-beliefs and assumptions;
  - relationships among thought, emotion, interpretation, and lived experience;
  - observing emotional life;
  - identifying recurring patterns and ways of engaging with them.
- Initial discovery source supplied by the user: https://www.uemet.com/ (verify exact pages and authorship before cataloging individual teachings).
- Research the method's original materials, vocabulary, process, examples, scope, and limitations. Do not infer equivalence with CBT or other clinical methods without evidence.

### Comparative study requirement
Create explicit cross-links among the three entries while preserving their boundaries:
1. Primary Hasidic teaching and its textual lineage (Baal Shem Tov).
2. Contemporary method developed on the basis of that teaching (עין הבדולח, Rabbi Yuval HaCohen Asherov).
3. Separate contemporary method for identifying inner statements and beliefs (נקודת אמת, Rabbi Asher Eliyahu Garlitz).
For each, retain author/source attribution, exact text or lesson reference, interpretation, practical exercise (if supplied by the source), and epistemic status. Compare similarities and differences without collapsing them into one doctrine.

## Required processing standard for every book, source, or method

A bibliographic entry alone is not a completed knowledge asset. Subject to rights and source availability, each item should move through separately tracked stages:
1. Verified bibliographic record and provenance.
2. Lawful source text / recording / lesson access, with edition and rights status.
3. Structured, in-depth summary that distinguishes the author's claims from the compiler's interpretation.
4. Concepts and definitions, with source locators.
5. Principles and arguments, including context and qualifications.
6. Examples and case illustrations actually present in the source; label any newly created examples as illustrative.
7. Reflection questions, clearly identified as derived prompts rather than quotations.
8. Cross-links to related and contrasting sources, preserving disagreements and different epistemic traditions.
9. Possible conversational applications, including when the approach may be relevant, when it may be unsuitable, and what it must not be used to conclude.
10. Quality review for fidelity, evidence status, rights compliance, and retrieval by exact source/page/section.

Track these stages independently. Never mark a source as processed, ingested, or retrievable merely because its URL was collected, its title was listed, or a summary was drafted.
